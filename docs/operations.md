# Phase 1 operations and technical design

This is the minimum shared model for the [Phase 1 business specification](phase-1.md) and its separate [consumer](consumer.md), [store](store.md) and [admin](admin.md) applications. The initial [PostgreSQL schema](../services/api/migrations/001_phase1.sql) creates the tables; this guide defines the behavior the backend must enforce, not a choice of Node.js framework.

## Order lifecycle

```text
Consumer/staff online: AWAITING_PAYMENT → PLACED (verified payment) / PAYMENT_FAILED (failed or expired)
Staff cash:            PLACED (cash collected and recorded at order entry)
PLACED → ACCEPTED → PREPARING → READY_FOR_PICKUP → COLLECTED
                            └→ READY_FOR_DELIVERY → OUT_FOR_DELIVERY → DELIVERED
PLACED / ACCEPTED / PREPARING / READY_* / OUT_FOR_DELIVERY → CANCELLED (staff reason required)
PLACED → CANCELLED (customer before acceptance)
```

`AWAITING_PAYMENT` and `PAYMENT_FAILED` are visible to the originating app and admin, but not actionable in the store fulfillment queue. `PLACED` requires either verified online payment or a staff-recorded cash collection at order entry. `READY_*` means either ready status, not an additional state. Pickup and delivery branches cannot be swapped after payment. Delivery assignment and handover happen outside the app. Grinding and packing are work within `PREPARING`, not extra statuses. Staff record status changes with actor and time. `PAYMENT_FAILED`, `COLLECTED`, `DELIVERED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal. Staff can cancel before handover with a reason, including when an out-for-delivery order returns undelivered; consumer-app cancellation is limited to its own `PLACED` orders. A paid cancellation starts a refund tracked separately from order status. After handover, returns/refunds follow an approved support policy. Admin configures opening/closing times per day, weekly closed days and an order cutoff in Nagpur local time; show the schedule in both Android apps, reject checkout when the schedule is missing or ordering is closed, and offer pickup only within open hours.

## Flour pickup and ready-time rules

- For a pickup order containing flour, select one preparation mode for the whole order: **grind in front of the customer** or **prepare before a requested pickup time**. Both consumer and staff checkout offer the same choices; delivery flour is prepared before dispatch. Grind texture (fine/medium/coarse) is a separate order-item choice. Do not require the choice for rice-only orders.
- In-front mode quotes an **approximately 30-minute wait beginning when staff record customer arrival**. Show the last arrival time allowed by closing and the preparation lead time; if the customer arrives later, staff arrange another time or cancel/refund. Do not mill before arrival or show a fixed ready timestamp before check-in; until then show “about 30 minutes after arrival.” After check-in, staff see the arrival time and a ready-time estimate, and start `PREPARING` when they begin work.
- Advance mode offers only pickup times within store hours that leave enough lead time for preparation; validate the requested time again at checkout and store it with the order. Staff start milling after acceptance and aim to mark the whole order `READY_FOR_PICKUP` by that time. If payment confirms too late or the store cannot meet the time, staff contact the customer to agree on an updated estimate or cancel/refund. Do not mark it ready solely because the clock reached the selected time.
- The paid store queue shows mode, arrival/requested time, current estimate and a delay indicator. Staff prioritize work against requested pickup times and recorded arrivals, update an estimate with actor/time and reason when capacity or a delay changes it, and contact staff-entered customers directly. Consumer-app customers see the updated estimate on refresh. Show pickup times in the Nagpur store's local time. The 30 minutes and requested pickup time are estimates until staff confirms readiness; no exact queue position or automatic scheduling engine is required.

## Checkout and stock rules

- Each sellable weight is a variant; rice age differentiates stocked rice variants. Flour grind is a required order-item choice, with the same variant stock and price for all grinds in Phase 1. Flour availability represents grain available to mill, not premilled flour; staff must keep this quantity aligned with physical supply.
- The server validates product/variant activity, quantities, serviceable pincode, prices, eligible points and delivery fee for **both** consumer and staff checkout. The cart is only an estimate. The server returns a quote that must be reconfirmed if anything changed before payment starts or cash is collected. Both checkouts capture customer name/mobile; staff checkout records the staff actor and a contact/address snapshot, but does not require a consumer account or silently attach an unverified phone number to one.
- Pending payment temporarily reserves stock with an expiry. Order creation and reservation occur atomically; concurrent checkouts cannot drive stock below zero. Release reservation on failed/expired payment. On cancellation return stock only if it is still sellable; after preparation, staff reconcile stock manually. Staff stock adjustments must not consume stock already reserved for orders.
- Store the product name, weight, rice age, flour grind, quantity, unit price, line total, delivery fee, points redeemed/value and final amount charged on the order. Past orders must not depend on current catalog values. Store the delivery address on each delivery order; pickup has none. Keep the flour pickup choice, requested time (if advance), arrival time (if in-front), current estimated ready time and actual ready time on the order; no extra fulfillment states are needed.
- Money uses decimal amounts in INR with explicit rounding rules and server-calculated totals. Show any legally required tax detail according to the confirmed tax policy; never infer a tax rate from product category alone.
- Use one idempotency key per checkout submission. A retry must return the existing order/payment state rather than creating another charge or cash receipt. Staff must confirm the server saved a cash order before re-entering it after connectivity failure; check the server/provider outcome before retrying an online payment. An order number appears only after persistence.

## Customer contact and reward points

- Require customer name and mobile on every order; consumer checkout verifies the mobile by OTP. Staff may enter a customer's name/mobile without the consumer app or OTP for an ordinary order. Ask separately for optional marketing consent, unchecked by default, and record the customer's choice, time and source. Order updates and points do not imply permission for marketing messages.
- Earn points only once when an order reaches `COLLECTED` or `DELIVERED`, using a business-approved rate. For staff-entered orders without a verified customer, keep the earned points unclaimed on that order until the mobile is verified by OTP in either app; only then link them to the verified customer's balance. Never attach an unverified typed number to another customer's account or credit points for `AWAITING_PAYMENT`, `PAYMENT_FAILED` or `CANCELLED` orders.
- Before quote confirmation, allow a verified customer to redeem points if their balance meets the configured minimum. The backend computes their value and maximum usable points, reserves the points for an active checkout, and returns them on failure/expiry/cancellation so a repeat or concurrent checkout cannot spend them twice. Keep a positive payable amount for cash/UPI/card in Phase 1. Staff redemption uses OTP in the store app; no consumer app installation is required.
- Record each earn, redeem, restore or approved refund adjustment once against its order, with points and time; show current balance from these records. If a completed order is later refunded, reverse its earned points according to the confirmed returns policy. Confirm earning rate, minimum redemption balance, point value, redemption limit and treatment of post-completion refunds before implementation; do not add tiers, expiry or campaigns in Phase 1.

## Payment and refunds

- Consumer or store staff starts UPI or card payment for the customer after confirming the server quote, including any points used. The backend creates a payment attempt for the remaining amount and INR currency; the customer uses provider-hosted checkout or an approved payment link/QR, without entering card details in the store app. Choose the gateway during implementation; no provider is committed by this specification. Neither app can mark an online payment `PAID`.
- Staff may instead collect the remaining cash **when entering the order**. The backend atomically records the order, exact cash amount, staff actor and collection time as `PAID`, then marks it `PLACED`. Cash collection is a staff attestation, subject to admin reconciliation; it is not a provider-verified transaction. Consumer-app checkout does not offer cash. No unpaid cash order enters the queue, and cash on pickup/delivery is not part of Phase 1.
- Verify provider webhook authenticity, provider transaction reference, amount, currency and order association on the backend. Apply each callback once, including duplicate or out-of-order delivery. A verified success changes the order to `PLACED` and exposes it to store staff; a failed/expired attempt changes it to `PAYMENT_FAILED` and releases reserved stock.
- A late payment success after reservation expiry needs a recorded refund/reconciliation path; it must not silently create an unfulfillable order. A customer retry after failure starts a new quote and attempt only after the previous outcome is known. Admin can inspect uncertain payments; the consumer sees `pending`, `paid` or `failed` based on backend state.
- Cancelling a paid order sets the payment's refund status to `PENDING`. Restore any redeemed points once; for online payment, initiate a provider refund for the amount charged once and record its result. For cash, admin records the manual return of the cash portion with amount, actor and time. Surface `REFUNDED` only after provider confirmation or manual cash refund confirmation, otherwise show a failure/pending action for admin. Never treat cancellation as proof that money was returned. Admin and store see refund state; consumer-app customers see the same state for their orders.
- Tax/receipt format, payment provider, fees and refund timing require business confirmation before launch. Do not store card data or provider credentials in application tables or logs.

## Minimal records

| Record | Required fields or relationships |
| --- | --- |
| Store | Name, address, contact, opening/closing times and closed days plus order cutoff in Nagpur local time, enabled delivery pincodes, flat delivery charge, active status/temporary closure. One store in Phase 1. |
| Customer | ID, verified mobile, name and points balance derived from reward transactions. |
| Staff account | ID, store, role (`ADMIN` or `STAFF`), password hash, active status. |
| Product/category | English product name/description, category, image reference, flour/rice type, active status. Only verified claims displayed. |
| Sellable variant | Product, weight/unit, rice age if applicable, selling price, available stock, active status. Unique combination of product, weight and rice age. |
| Catalog translation | Product/category reference, language code (`en`, `hi`, `mr`), translated name/description. English fallback for missing display content. |
| UI translation | Key, language code, translated text and version for the three apps; bundled English fallback if translation API is unavailable. UI keys are maintained with releases, not edited as arbitrary admin content. |
| Order | Optional verified customer reference, source (`CONSUMER`/`STORE`), creating staff actor for store orders, customer name/mobile snapshot, store, order number, pickup/delivery type, address snapshot if delivery, flour pickup mode/requested time/arrival/estimated and actual ready times where applicable, status, item subtotal, delivery charge, points applied/value, amount charged, timestamps, cancellation reason when applicable, checkout idempotency key. |
| Order item | Order, product and variant references, name/weight/age and translated-name snapshots at purchase, grind choice for flour, quantity, unit price and line total. |
| Payment | Order, method (`CASH`/`UPI`/`CARD`), exact amount/currency and `PENDING`/`PAID`/`FAILED` status. Online attempts have provider/reference, idempotency key and expiry; the single cash collection has staff actor and collection time. Refund state/reference or manual cash refund actor/time belongs to the paid payment. |
| Reward transaction | Verified customer or unclaimed staff order, source order, points and action (earn, redeem, restore, adjustment), time and unique action/order key so retries cannot duplicate it. |
| Marketing choice | Customer/order reference, opt-in or opt-out, time and capture source; kept separate from payment and rewards. |
| Status event | Order, from/to status, actor, time; provides a simple audit trail and customer history. |

Restrict personal data and reward balances by role and ownership. Each app calls the **same backend APIs**, which own this single PostgreSQL database; no app queries the database directly. Snapshot names/prices/choices on orders so edits or translations do not rewrite history. No courier, coupon, banner, loyalty-tier, review, wishlist or address-book tables are required for Phase 1.

## Deployment and security baseline

- Confirmed stack: one Node.js API, a React admin web panel, separate consumer and store Android apps built with Kotlin/Jetpack Compose, and one PostgreSQL database on a private network. Only the API connects to PostgreSQL. Select the Node.js framework and hosting details during implementation; the older Java/Spring proposal is superseded.
- Expose only the public API/web entrypoint over HTTPS. A reverse proxy can terminate TLS, route web/API traffic and enforce request-size limits where needed; keep the backend and database unreachable from the public internet. Do not add a proxy solely to introduce another service if the host already provides these controls.
- Phone OTP must use an approved provider, short expiry and request/verification limits; do not log codes. Staff/admin passwords are hashed, sessions are revocable, and role/store permissions are checked on the server. Admin web sessions use secure, HttpOnly cookies with appropriate SameSite and CSRF protection; Android credentials use secure device storage and short-lived server-issued tokens.
- Validate and authorize every order/reward read/write; never trust client totals, points balances, statuses, online payment outcomes or role claims. Limit cash-paid order creation to authenticated staff, record their identity, and reconcile cash receipts and refunds. Verify provider callbacks server-side, limit login and checkout abuse, use parameterized database access, store secrets outside source control and avoid phone/address details in application logs. Send marketing only to customers with recorded opt-in; allow them to withdraw it without affecting orders or points.
- Back up the database, test restoration, and restrict operator access before launch. Retention/deletion policy for customer contact and addresses must be settled with the business. If product images are uploaded, validate file type/size and serve from controlled storage rather than an executable directory.

Interfaces can stay small: catalog/translations; OTP/session; consumer and staff quote/checkout with points; payment attempt and verified callback or staff cash collection; customer order/reward views; staff order entry/list/transition; admin catalog/stock/store/refund/reward settings. Define exact endpoints and subsequent migrations when implementation begins.
